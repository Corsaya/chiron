# The Chiron Principles

Chiron is a Personal Intelligence System for Performance.

It helps people answer:

> Given who I want to become, what is the most meaningful thing to do next—and why?

Chiron makes the next step clear, shows why it matters, and makes earned progress visible.

## 1. Users define what matters

People choose their aspirations, goals, and commitments. They may change them freely. Chiron connects actions to those intentions without judging whether someone has earned an identity.

Enjoyment, curiosity, rest, and necessary everyday care deserve space without having to justify themselves as performance improvements. Life is the context for every domain.

## 2. Evidence grounds performance claims

Keep observations, interpretations, and proposals distinct: what was recorded, what it might mean, and what to do next.

An observation is a record, not an infallible truth. Preserve its source and context, distinguish self-report from measurement, and allow corrections. Missing evidence is not evidence of failure.

## 3. Interpretations remain inspectable

Explain how a conclusion follows from its evidence. Make uncertainty, assumptions, and unknowns visible. New evidence may change an interpretation without changing what was originally observed.

Never present an estimate as a measurement or an inference as an established fact.

## 4. Journeys provide direction

Journeys connect aspirations and goals to capabilities, activities, and meaningful checkpoints. They can branch, overlap, revisit foundations, and reach a conclusion.

Capabilities connect journeys across domains. Evidence of a capability in one context does not automatically demonstrate it in another. Progress follows the person and the evidence, not a compulsory sequence.

## 5. Mission Control proposes, never commands

Mission Control describes contextual guidance within existing navigation, not a competing dashboard. It helps the user choose a useful, feasible next action within the whole of their day. It respects available time, existing commitments, recovery, and competing priorities.

Lenses change what receives attention without silently changing commitments or concealing important conflicts. Users can adjust, postpone, replace, or decline a proposal.

## 6. The Coach explains before recommending

The Coach makes its observations and reasoning available alongside its advice. It helps users exercise judgment and asks when essential context is missing.

No hidden replanning, material assumptions, or silent edits. Changes to commitments require user direction or explicit standing authorization within understood limits. Authorized changes remain visible and reviewable.

## 7. Progress is earned, not assumed

Celebrate specific, supported accomplishments. Distinguish participation, consistency, and demonstrated capability. Do not turn points, streaks, or confident language into claims of mastery.

Personal worth is never a performance metric. Difficult sessions deserve useful direction. Rest and changed priorities are not moral failures.

Memory gives evidence continuity: meaningful moments, revisits, and changes become a story the user can recognize and correct. Distinguish personal reflection from inferred narrative, link claims to their evidence, and never invent a turning point or erase setbacks to make a better story.

## 8. Every recommendation can answer “Why?”

A user can inspect what prompted a recommendation, which goal it serves, what remains uncertain, and what would change it. When advice rests on a general principle rather than personal evidence, say so.

Explanations should help people decide, not merely persuade them to comply.

## 9. Domains share one core model

Aspirations, goals, journeys, capabilities, activities, evidence, interpretations, and proposals connect the experience across domains. Each domain retains its own meaning of performance.

Learning, training, building, and managing commitments need different measures. Shared presentation must not imply that those measures are interchangeable. The whole person takes precedence over any one domain's priorities.

## 10. Intelligence augments judgment

Chiron helps people understand their situation and act with greater agency. Users retain control over their commitments, personal history, and the meaning they assign to their growth.

Every actionable experience should make three things understandable: Where am I? Why am I here? What is the next useful step?

## 11. Users own their data

Chiron is local-first and built around durable, portable records. Users can inspect, export, edit, and preserve their information independently of any intelligence provider or cloud service. Intelligence enhances user-owned knowledge rather than replacing it.

Users choose what leaves their device and for what purpose. Their records remain useful when an optional service is unavailable or disconnected.

## How these principles guide decisions

Evaluate every proposed feature and design decision against these principles before considering implementation. It should help users understand, choose, act, reflect, or trust the experience. Necessary foundations should support those same purposes.

These principles are frozen as the product baseline. Changing them is exceptional and requires an explicit explanation of what changes, why it serves users, and which promise it affects. They must never drift silently to accommodate a convenient feature.
